The short version

  • Older systems (installed before about 2010) likely use R-22 ("Freon"). It hasn't been made or imported in the US since 2020, so the only supply is recycled, and it's expensive.
  • Most systems installed from about 2010 through 2024 use R-410A ("Puron"). These systems are legal to keep, repair, and recharge.
  • New systems made since January 1, 2025 use lower-impact refrigerants, mainly R-454B or R-32.

Not sure which you have? It's printed on the outdoor unit's label. Send us a photo and we'll tell you.

Timeline

WhenWhat changed
2010New AC equipment could no longer be made to use R-22. The industry moved to R-410A.
Jan 1, 2020US production and import of R-22 ended. Only recovered and recycled R-22 remains for servicing older systems.
Dec 2020Congress passed the AIM Act, phasing down HFC refrigerants like R-410A by 85% by 2036.
Jan 1, 2023New federal efficiency standards (SEER2). In the Southwest, including Arizona, new split-system AC under 3.5 tons must be at least 14.3 SEER2, the strictest region in the country.
Jan 1, 2025Under the EPA's Technology Transitions rule, new home AC and heat pump systems must use refrigerants with a global warming potential under 700. That's the switch to R-454B and R-32.
2025A shortage of R-454B cylinders drove up refrigerant and install costs. It had largely eased by late 2025.
May 21, 2026The EPA removed the January 1, 2026 deadline for installing leftover R-410A systems built before 2025 (effective July 2026). That remaining inventory can still be installed until it runs out.

What it means for you

If you have an R-410A system

You're fine. Keeping it, repairing it, and recharging it are all legal. But R-410A supply is shrinking on purpose: the HFC phasedown is in its 40% reduction step now (2024–2028) and steps down to a 70% reduction in 2029. Expect refrigerant to keep getting more expensive. If your system leaks, fixing the leak beats topping it off every summer.

If you have an R-22 system

A big refrigerant leak on an R-22 system is usually the moment to replace. Recycled R-22 is pricey, and these systems are 15+ years old. A smaller repair that doesn't touch the refrigerant can still make sense.

If you're replacing

  • New A2L systems (R-454B, R-32) are the new standard. A2L means "mildly flammable": hard to ignite, and installed with extra safety measures by trained techs. They're safe in your home.
  • Leftover R-410A systems are still legal to install, thanks to the 2026 change, and can sometimes cost less up front. Weigh that against R-410A's rising service costs over the system's life.
  • Refrigerants can't be mixed. You can't put R-454B into an R-410A system. A new outdoor unit usually means a matched indoor coil too.
  • Efficiency: compare SEER2 ratings. Anything new sold for Arizona meets the Southwest minimum, and higher SEER2 saves more in our long cooling season.

What's next

When the EPA loosened the rules in 2026, it noted that the resulting increase in HFC demand "will likely cause future regulations restricting refrigerants." The bigger fixed date is the HFC phasedown's step to a 70% reduction in 2029. We'll keep this page updated as rules change.

REFRIGERANT IS NEVER DIY

Buying and handling refrigerant for home AC requires EPA Section 608 certification. If you hear hissing or see oily spots on the copper lines, call us. Plenty of other jobs are fair game, though: see our DIY guides.
