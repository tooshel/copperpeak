Heating repair, tune-ups & replacement

Arizona winters are short, which is exactly why heaters get ignored until the first cold night. We fix gas furnaces, electric furnaces, and heat pumps across the East Valley, and we'll tell you straight whether a repair is worth it.

Heating repair

When your heat quits, we find the actual cause, explain what's going on in plain English, and give you options, from the simplest repair to replacement, so you can pick what fits your home and budget. No pressure, no surprise charges.

Signs your heater needs attention

  • Blowing cool or lukewarm air when it's set to heat
  • Turning on and off every few minutes (short cycling)
  • A burning smell that lasts more than the first few minutes of the season. A brief dusty smell on the first cold day is normal.
  • Banging, rattling, or squealing from the furnace or air handler
  • Heat pump outdoor unit iced over for hours, or running nonstop
  • Electric bills spiking in winter, often a sign the backup heat strips are doing all the work
GAS FURNACE SAFETY

If you smell gas (rotten eggs), leave the house and call your gas utility from outside. Every home with a gas furnace should have working carbon monoxide detectors. If one goes off, get everyone out and call 911.

Systems we work on

  • Heat pumps, the most common heating in the Valley. One system cools in summer and runs in reverse to heat in winter.
  • Gas furnaces, including ignition, flame sensors, inducers, and safety controls
  • Electric furnaces and heat strips in air handlers
  • Package units, the all-in-one rooftop and ground units common in Arizona homes
  • Ductless mini-splits

We service all major brands, including Carrier, Bryant, Trane, American Standard, Lennox, Rheem, Goodman, Amana, York, Payne, Frigidaire, Maytag, Gibson, and many more.

Heating tune-ups

Most manufacturers expect yearly maintenance to keep a warranty valid, and utilities recommend twice-a-year checkups on heat pumps since the same equipment runs all year. A fall tune-up catches the weak capacitor, dirty flame sensor, or failing igniter before the first cold night. We check electrical connections, safety controls, airflow, and, on gas units, combustion and venting.

Repair or replace?

We'll give you the honest numbers. A repair usually makes sense on a system that's under 10–12 years old with a single failed part. Replacement starts making sense when the repair is a big share of the cost of a new system, the system is 15+ years old, or repairs keep stacking up. Either way, it's your call, and we'll explain both options.
