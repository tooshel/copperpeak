Where's the filter?

  • In a return grille: the large vent on a wall or ceiling, usually in a hallway. Open the grille's clips or screws.
  • At the air handler or furnace: in a slot or door near where the big duct connects. In Arizona that's often in a closet, garage, or attic.
  • Both: some homes have more than one. Check them all.

What you need

  • The right size filter. It's printed on the edge of the old one, like 20 × 25 × 1.
  • A screwdriver if the grille is screwed shut

Steps

  1. Turn the system Off at the thermostat so it isn't pulling air while the filter is out.
  2. Open the grille or filter door and slide the old filter out. Note the arrow on its edge.
  3. Insert the new filter with the airflow arrow pointing toward the unit, away from the room and into the duct.
  4. Close the grille or door, then turn the system back on.
  5. Write the date on the new filter's edge so you know when it went in.

Which filter to buy

MERV ratingGood for
MERV 6–8Basic protection for the system, catches dust and lint
MERV 9–11The sweet spot for most homes: pollen, pet dander, fine dust
MERV 13Allergies and smoke. Fine in most systems, but watch for reduced airflow.
THICKER ISN'T ALWAYS BETTER

A very dense 1-inch filter can choke airflow, especially on older systems, and choking airflow causes freezing and overheating. If you want high-MERV filtration, ask us about a 4-inch media cabinet. It filters more and restricts airflow less.

How often to change it

  • 1-inch filters: check monthly in summer and replace every 1–3 months. In a hot summer with dust storms, monthly is common.
  • 4-inch media filters: usually every 6–12 months.
  • Change sooner with pets, construction nearby, or right after a haboob.

If you can't see light through it, it's done.
