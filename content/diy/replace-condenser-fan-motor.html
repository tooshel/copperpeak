ADVANCED JOB: READ THIS FIRST

You'll be working inside a 240-volt unit with a charged capacitor and a heavy, sharp fan blade. Kill the power at the breaker and disconnect, verify zero volts, and discharge the capacitor (see the capacitor guide) before you start. If you haven't done a capacitor swap before, start there.

Signs the fan motor is bad

  • The fan doesn't spin, and a new capacitor didn't fix it
  • The fan spins slowly, stops and starts, or the motor is very hot to the touch
  • Grinding, squealing, or rattling from the top of the unit
  • The fan blade wobbles or has play on the shaft (worn bearings)

Match the new motor

Read the old motor's label, or send us a photo and we'll match it for you. The new motor needs:

SpecExampleWhy it matters
Horsepower1/4 HPToo small overheats; too large wastes power.
Voltage208–230VMust match the unit.
RPM825 or 1075Wrong speed means wrong airflow across the coil.
RotationCCW (shaft end)Backwards pushes air the wrong way. Some motors are reversible.
Shaft size1/2 inchHas to fit the fan blade hub.
Run capacitor5 µFThe new motor may call for a different value.

Replace the capacitor along with the motor, or at least the fan side of a dual capacitor. A weak capacitor shortens a new motor's life.

What you need

  • Matching motor and capacitor
  • Multimeter, insulated screwdrivers, nut drivers, and Allen wrenches for the set screw
  • Penetrating oil, for a rusted blade hub
  • Wire nuts, zip ties, a tape measure, and your phone camera

Steps

  1. Kill and verify the power, and discharge the capacitor.
  2. Photograph the motor wiring at the capacitor and contactor, and note the wire colors and where each one lands.
  3. Disconnect the motor wires and feed them out of the control box, noting how they route up to the top.
  4. Remove the top grille. The motor and blade usually hang from it. Lift carefully and set it upside down on cardboard. Don't hang it by the wires.
  5. Measure the blade position: the distance from the blade hub to the end of the shaft, or to the grille. The new blade has to sit at the same height.
  6. Remove the blade: loosen the set screw, add penetrating oil if it's stuck, and work it off evenly. Don't hammer on the blade.
  7. Swap the motor on the grille with the same mounting bolts, and keep any rain shield.
  8. Reinstall the blade at your measured height, with the set screw tightened on the flat of the shaft.
  9. Route and connect the wires to match your photos, add the new capacitor, and zip-tie the wiring away from the blade.
  10. Reinstall the grille, spin the blade by hand to make sure nothing rubs, then restore power and test. Air should blow up and out of the top.
CALL A PRO IF
  • Your unit has a variable-speed (ECM) fan motor, common on high-efficiency systems. Those need programming and aren't DIY-friendly.
  • The compressor overheated or won't run after the fan is fixed
  • You find burned wiring or a damaged coil
  • You'd rather not juggle a heavy grille and blade on a roof or in a tight side yard
