--- title: How to Clean Your AC Coils, Inside and Out description: Clean the outdoor condenser coil and the indoor evaporator coil safely. Arizona dust and monsoon storms clog coils fast; here's the right cleaner, the right water pressure, and when to stop. summary: Dusty coils make your AC work harder and cool worse. Rinse the outdoor coil yourself; clean the indoor coil if you can reach it safely. level: Moderate time: 1 hour order: 1 date: 2026-10-25 lead: Your AC has two coils. The outdoor one (the condenser) dumps heat outside, and the indoor one (the evaporator) pulls heat out of your house. Coat either one with dust and your system runs longer, cools worse, and works its compressor harder. In the Valley, dust storms clog outdoor coils fast. ---
POWER OFF FIRST

Turn the thermostat Off, switch off the AC breaker, and pull the outdoor disconnect (the gray box on the wall by the unit). Never spray water into a unit that has power.

Part 1: The outdoor coil (condenser)

What you need

  • Garden hose with an adjustable nozzle (no pressure washer)
  • Foaming condenser coil cleaner, a non-acid type made for home units
  • A soft brush and a fin comb (optional, for bent fins)
  • Gloves and safety glasses

Steps

  1. Kill the power at the thermostat, breaker, and disconnect (see above).
  2. Clear around the unit. Pull leaves, trash, and weeds from the base, and trim plants back at least 2 feet on all sides. The unit needs room to breathe.
  3. Brush off loose debris gently with a soft brush, in the direction of the fins (usually up and down), never across them.
  4. Apply the coil cleaner to the outside of the coil per the label and let it foam and soak, usually 5–10 minutes. Keep it out of the electrical box.
  5. Rinse with gentle pressure, spraying straight into the fins, top to bottom. A pressure washer flattens the fins, so stick to a garden hose. Rinse until the water runs clear.
  6. Straighten bent fins with a fin comb if they're flattened in spots.
  7. Let it dry 30 minutes, then restore power: disconnect, breaker, thermostat.
ARIZONA TIMING

Clean the outdoor coil in spring before the heat hits, and again after monsoon season or any big dust storm.

Part 2: The indoor coil (evaporator)

The indoor coil sits above the furnace or inside the air handler, often in a closet, garage, or attic. On many systems it's sealed behind sheet metal with no easy access. If that's yours, this part is a job for us.

What you need

  • No-rinse evaporator coil cleaner, a foaming spray that drains out with the condensate
  • A screwdriver or nut driver for the access panel
  • Flashlight and gloves
  • Foil HVAC tape, to reseal the panel if it was taped

Steps

  1. Turn off the power at the thermostat and the indoor unit's breaker (or the furnace service switch).
  2. Open the coil access panel. Only if it's held by screws or tape. Don't cut into ductwork or insulation.
  3. Look before you spray. Light dust is cleaner territory. A thick felt-like mat of dirt means the filter has been failing, so clean the coil and then fix the filter.
  4. Spray the no-rinse cleaner evenly on the coil face. It foams, dissolves the grime, and drains out through the condensate line once the AC runs.
  5. Check the drain pan and line while you're in there. See clearing the drain line.
  6. Close and seal the panel. Re-tape any seams so the system isn't pulling in attic air, then restore power and run the AC so the cleaner drains away.
NO HOUSEHOLD CLEANERS

Don't use bleach, ammonia, or degreasers on coils. They corrode the aluminum fins and copper tubing, and corroded coils leak refrigerant.

CALL A PRO IF
  • The indoor coil is sealed in and you'd have to cut or unscrew ductwork to reach it
  • The coil is matted solid. It may need to come out for a deep clean.
  • You see oily spots on the coil or tubing, a sign of a refrigerant leak
  • Fins are flattened across large areas
