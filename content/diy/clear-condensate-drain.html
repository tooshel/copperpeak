Clear a clogged condensate drain line with vinegar and a wet/dry vac, reset the float switch, and prevent algae clogs in Arizona. summary: Water in the pan or an AC that shut itself off? A clogged drain line is a classic, and vinegar plus a shop vac usually fixes it. level: Easy time: 30 min order: 3 date: 2026-10-25 lead: Your AC pulls water out of the air, and that water has to drain somewhere. When algae and dust clog the line, water backs up, a safety switch shuts the AC off, or worse, it overflows onto your ceiling. That's especially common with attic units here in the Valley. ---

Signs of a clogged drain

  • The AC shut off and won't restart, but everything else looks fine
  • Water in the pan under the air handler, or dripping from a pipe over a window or eave
  • Water stains on the ceiling under an attic unit
  • Musty smell from the vents

What you need

  • A wet/dry shop vac
  • Distilled white vinegar (about a cup)
  • Towels and a flashlight
  • Duct tape or a rag, to seal the vac hose to the pipe

Steps

  1. Turn the system off at the thermostat, and at the breaker if you'll be working near the air handler's wiring.
  2. Find the drain line. It's a PVC pipe (usually ¾-inch) leaving the indoor unit, often with a T-fitting and a removable cap near the unit. That's the cleanout.
  3. Empty any standing water from the drain pan with the shop vac or towels.
  4. Vacuum from the outside end. Find where the line exits the house, often near the outdoor unit or a foundation wall. Seal the shop vac hose over it with tape or a rag and run it for a minute or two. You'll often hear the clog let go.
  5. Flush with vinegar. Pour about a cup of distilled white vinegar into the cleanout, let it sit 30 minutes, then flush with a few cups of water. It should drain freely.
  6. Reset the safety switch. Many float switches reset on their own once the water's gone. If yours has a reset button, press it. Then turn the system back on.

Keep it from coming back

  • Pour a cup of vinegar into the cleanout every month or two from May through September, when the AC is making the most water.
  • Replace your air filter on schedule. Dust that gets past the filter feeds the gunk in the pan.
  • Ask us about a UV lamp for the coil and pan if clogs keep coming back. See Air Quality.
CALL A PRO IF
  • The drain pan is rusted through or cracked
  • The line runs inside a wall and you can't reach either end
  • Water has already stained a ceiling. Check for hidden damage and mold.
  • It clogs again within a few weeks, which can mean a sagging or badly sloped line
