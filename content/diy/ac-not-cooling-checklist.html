Check these 8 things first: thermostat, breakers, the outdoor disconnect, the filter, ice, the drain safety switch, and the outdoor fan. summary: Thermostat, breakers, filter, ice, drain switch. Most no-cool calls start with one of these, and several are free fixes. level: Easy time: 15 min order: 1 date: 2026-10-25 lead: Before you pay for a service call, spend fifteen minutes on this list. A surprising number of "my AC died" calls turn out to be a tripped breaker, a clogged filter, or a full drain pan. ---

1. Check the thermostat

Make sure it's set to Cool (not Heat or Fan), the set temperature is below the room temperature, and any schedule isn't overriding you. If the screen is blank or dim, replace the batteries. A thermostat with dead batteries can't call for cooling.

2. Check both breakers

Your system has two: one for the indoor unit (air handler or furnace) and one for the outdoor unit, usually a large two-pole breaker labeled AC or Condenser. If one is tripped, switch it fully off, then back on.

STOP IF IT TRIPS AGAIN

Reset a breaker once. If it trips again, leave it off and call. A breaker that keeps tripping is protecting you from an electrical fault, often a shorted compressor or motor.

3. Check the outdoor disconnect

Next to the outdoor unit there's a small gray box on the wall: the disconnect. Make sure its pull-out block is fully seated or its lever is on. People bump these, and techs occasionally forget to put them back.

4. Check the air filter

A clogged filter starves the system of air. It cools poorly, and eventually the indoor coil freezes. If you can't see light through it, replace it. See how to change your air filter.

5. Look for ice

Check the large copper line at the outdoor unit and, if you can see it, the indoor coil. Ice means the system froze, usually from low airflow (filter) or low refrigerant.

  1. Set the thermostat to Off and the fan to On. The fan melts the ice faster.
  2. Wait until it's fully thawed. That can take several hours. Watch for melt water.
  3. Replace the filter if it's dirty, then switch back to Cool.

If it freezes again, call. Repeated freezing usually means a refrigerant problem, and that's a job for a certified tech.

6. Check the drain safety switch

Many systems have a float switch that shuts the AC off when the condensate drain backs up. It protects your ceiling, but it looks exactly like a dead AC. If there's water in the drain pan or the safety switch has tripped, clear the line: how to clear a clogged AC drain line.

7. Watch the outdoor unit

With the thermostat calling for cooling, go outside:

  • Fan spinning and unit humming: it's running. Make sure the coil isn't caked with dust or blocked by plants: how to clean your AC coils.
  • Humming but the fan isn't spinning: very often a failed capacitor. Don't poke the fan with your hand.
  • Completely silent: check the breaker and disconnect again. If both are on, the contactor or the thermostat wiring may be the issue.

8. Give it a fair test

On a 115° afternoon, a healthy system may only hold the house in the high 70s, and the air at the vents should be roughly 15–20°F cooler than the air going into the return. If the system runs but can't get there, it's time for a pro to check the charge and airflow.

CALL A PRO IF
  • A breaker trips more than once
  • You smell burning, or see melted or scorched wires
  • The system keeps freezing after you've changed the filter
  • You hear hissing or see oily spots on the copper lines (a refrigerant leak)
