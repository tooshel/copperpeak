--- title: How to Replace an AC Contactor description: Outdoor unit won't turn on, won't turn off, or buzzes? It may be the contactor, often fried by ants in Arizona. How to diagnose and replace an AC contactor safely. summary: The switch that turns your outdoor unit on and off. When it fails, the unit won't start or won't stop. Ants are a classic Arizona cause. level: Moderate time: 45 min order: 3 date: 2026-10-25 lead: The contactor is a heavy-duty switch in the outdoor unit. When the thermostat calls for cooling, a low-voltage signal pulls it closed and sends 240 volts to the compressor and fan. Its contacts wear out, burn, and in Arizona, get jammed by ants that crawl in and get crushed. ---
240 VOLTS AND A STORED CHARGE

The line side of the contactor carries full power until the breaker and disconnect are off, and the capacitor next to it can still hold a charge. Kill the power, verify it with a meter, and discharge the capacitor. The steps are in our capacitor guide.

Signs of a bad contactor

  • The thermostat calls for cooling, the indoor fan runs, but the outdoor unit is silent
  • The outdoor unit won't shut off, even with the thermostat off. The contacts can weld shut. Turn the breaker off until it's fixed.
  • Loud buzzing or chattering from the outdoor unit
  • Contacts that look black, pitted, or melted, or a nest of dead ants inside

What you need

  • The replacement contactor. Match the specs below, or send us a photo of the old one's label.
  • Multimeter
  • Insulated screwdrivers and a nut driver
  • Capacitor discharge tool or resistor
  • Phone camera and masking tape

Match these specs

  • Coil voltage: almost always 24 VAC on home systems
  • Poles: single-pole or double-pole. Match what you have.
  • Amp rating (FLA, such as 30A or 40A): equal or higher than the original

Steps

  1. Kill the power: thermostat off, AC breaker off, disconnect pulled.
  2. Open the electrical panel and verify zero volts across the incoming wires with your meter.
  3. Discharge the capacitor before your hands go near anything.
  4. Photograph the wiring from a couple of angles and label every wire. The two thin wires are the 24V control wires from the thermostat.
  5. Remove the old contactor: unscrew it from the panel, then move the wires one at a time to the same positions on the new one. Line-side wires go to the line side, load-side to the load side.
  6. Mount the new contactor and tug each wire to make sure it's tight.
  7. Restore power and set the thermostat to Cool. You should hear a solid clunk as it closes, then the fan and compressor start. Turn the thermostat off and make sure everything stops.
KEEP THE ANTS OUT

Ants are drawn to the electrical field and pile up in the contacts. Keep vegetation and mulch away from the unit, and use an ant bait around the pad (not inside the electrical box).

CALL A PRO IF
  • The wires or terminal blocks are burned
  • The new contactor burns out quickly. Something upstream, like a shorted compressor or a thermostat wiring fault, is killing it.
  • There's no 24V signal from the thermostat at the coil. That's a control problem, not the contactor.
